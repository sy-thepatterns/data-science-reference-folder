# Reinforcement Learning

## Definition

Reinforcement learning studies agents that choose actions in an environment to maximize long-run reward from evaluative, often delayed feedback.

## Learning Signal

Rewards and transitions generated by interaction; the agent generally does not receive a correct action label.

## Data Structure

Training data must be partitioned according to the unit that will generalize: examples, users, time, environments, or tasks. Any statistics learned during preprocessing belong inside the training split.

## Formal Setup

A Markov decision process is $$(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,R,\gamma)$$. A policy $$\pi(a\mid s)$$ maximizes $$J(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^t r_t]$$.

## Typical Objective

Value-based, policy-based, model-based, and actor–critic algorithms optimize or approximate this control objective in different ways.

## Evaluation

Use held-out units that reproduce deployment conditions. Compare against a simple baseline, report uncertainty across splits or seeds, and measure data, label, compute, and latency costs when relevant.

## Strengths

Directly represents sequential consequences and delayed rewards.

## Limitations

Sample inefficiency, unsafe exploration, reward misspecification, partial observability, nonstationarity, and difficult off-policy evaluation.
